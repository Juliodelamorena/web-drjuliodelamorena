Treatments

PRP (platelet-rich plasma)

PRP (platelet-rich plasma) is a treatment obtained from the patient's own blood. A small blood sample is taken and processed to concentrate the platelets and the growth factors they contain, which are then applied to the injured area, usually under ultrasound guidance. It is used in selected cases of mild or moderate osteoarthritis and of chronic tendinopathies and injuries, when exercise and conservative treatment have not been enough. Each case is assessed first in clinic with examination and ultrasound. Dr Julio De la Morena applies it at the Regenerative Therapies and Sports Medicine Unit of Hospital Memorial Publio Cordón (Pozuelo de Alarcón, Madrid).

What it is used for

  • Osteoarthritis: mild or moderate, of the knee and in some cases the hip or ankle.
  • Chronic tendinopathies: tennis elbow, patellar tendon, Achilles tendon, plantar fascia and rotator cuff.
  • Some selected muscle and ligament injuries.

It does not replace therapeutic exercise: it works best as part of a complete plan.

How we work, step by step

  1. 1

    Assessment and indication

    A consultation with examination and ultrasound. PRP is not the first step: it is indicated when exercise and conservative treatment have not been enough, as European consensus statements recommend. We explain why it makes sense in your case and what improvement is realistic to expect.

  2. 2

    A preparation chosen for your injury

    Four tubes of blood, about 36 ml, are drawn and processed in a closed, sterile system. We work with two systems: Endoret, by BTI, developed in Spain, leukocyte-free and backed by more than two hundred scientific publications, and the Stryker system, which produces a preparation containing leukocytes. In each case we choose the one that best suits the injury.

  3. 3

    Always guided by ultrasound

    The injection is always performed while watching the needle and the target structure on screen, together with Dr Sergio Serrano, an interventional radiologist who has worked with the Real Madrid medical services for more than ten years. This ensures the treatment reaches exactly where it needs to act.

  4. 4

    A schedule that adapts

    The usual course is three sessions one week apart, but the schedule is guided by your clinical progress and what the ultrasound shows at each session.

  5. 5

    A complete treatment

    Depending on the injury, treatment is combined with a home exercise programme, physiotherapy or a biomechanical gait assessment. We coordinate with your physiotherapist or put you in touch with our physiotherapy and podiatry partners.

  6. 6

    Review and next step

    We review you at six weeks, or sooner if needed, measuring pain and function with validated scales. If the improvement is not as expected, your case is discussed at a clinical meeting with the hospital's orthopaedic specialists to decide the next step together.

What the research says

Mild or moderate knee osteoarthritis is the indication with the strongest support: a 2024 European consensus and several reviews of clinical trials show greater and longer-lasting relief than with hyaluronic acid or corticosteroids. There are also favourable results in gluteal tendinopathy, tennis elbow and plantar fasciitis. Not every trial has been positive, which is why careful patient selection matters. Summary of the studies with links.

When it is not appropriate

There are situations in which it should not be used, such as some blood disorders, infections or certain medications. That is why a prior assessment is always carried out in clinic, with your medical history and an examination.

Frequently asked questions

What exactly is PRP?

PRP stands for platelet-rich plasma: a platelet concentrate obtained from the patient's own blood. Applied to the injured area, the platelets release growth factors, proteins involved in tissue repair.

Is it painful?

Taking the blood is like a routine blood test. The application causes brief discomfort and there may be local soreness for one or two days.

How many sessions are needed?

The usual course is three sessions one week apart, always guided by ultrasound. The schedule is adjusted according to progress and what the ultrasound shows, and the result is reviewed at six weeks.

Does it work for everyone?

No. Some patients respond very well and others do not. That is why it is important to use it in the right cases and combine it with therapeutic exercise.

Would you like your case assessed?

A full assessment and a clear plan at your first visit; ultrasound and treatment the same day when indicated. The first consultation does not commit you to treatment: if no treatment is indicated, we will tell you.
