Conditions

Shoulder pain

The most common cause of shoulder pain is a rotator cuff problem, the group of tendons that move and stabilise the joint: tendinopathy, partial tears or bursitis. Other causes are adhesive capsulitis (frozen shoulder) and osteoarthritis. Pain when raising the arm, getting dressed or lying on that side at night is typical. Dynamic ultrasound in clinic is the ideal test for the shoulder, and in many cases treatment does not require surgery. Dr Julio De la Morena assesses it at Hospital Memorial Publio Cordón (Pozuelo de Alarcón, Madrid).

Common symptoms

  • Pain when raising the arm or reaching behind you.
  • Night pain that wakes you when you turn onto that shoulder.
  • Weakness when lifting objects overhead.
  • Progressive stiffness and loss of movement, in frozen shoulder.

Diagnosis

The examination identifies which structure hurts, and ultrasound shows the rotator cuff tendons moving, detecting tears, calcifications or bursitis on the spot, without waiting for another appointment.

Treatment

  • Therapeutic exercise to regain strength and control of the shoulder.
  • Ultrasound-guided injections into the bursa or joint when pain prevents progress.
  • PRP (platelet-rich plasma) in selected tendinopathies and partial tears.
  • Surgical assessment for complete tears in active patients or when conservative treatment does not work.

Frequently asked questions

Does a rotator cuff tear always need surgery?

No. Many partial and degenerative tears respond well to conservative treatment. Surgery is considered for complete traumatic tears in active people or when treatment does not work.

Why does my shoulder hurt more at night?

Lying down, the pressure on the shoulder and the position of the arm increase tension on the irritated tendons and bursa. It is a very typical symptom of rotator cuff problems.

Would you like your case assessed?

A full assessment and a clear plan at your first visit; ultrasound and treatment the same day when indicated. The first consultation does not commit you to treatment: if no treatment is indicated, we will tell you.
