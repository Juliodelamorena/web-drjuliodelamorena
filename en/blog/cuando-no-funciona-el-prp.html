When does PRP not work?

PRP (platelet-rich plasma) has evidence behind it in mild and moderate knee osteoarthritis and in some chronic tendinopathies. But it does not work equally well in every case, and knowing when not to recommend it matters as much as knowing when to. These are the situations in which Dr Julio De la Morena, at Hospital Memorial Publio Cordón (Pozuelo de Alarcón, Madrid), usually advises against it.

1. When osteoarthritis is very advanced

In a joint where the cartilage has practically disappeared, bone rubs on bone and the knee or hip has become deformed, the expected benefit of PRP is much smaller. The studies with good results mainly include mild and moderate osteoarthritis. In very advanced osteoarthritis it is better to talk plainly about other options.

2. When the pain comes from somewhere else

Knee pain can come from the hip; shoulder pain from the neck; buttock pain from the lower back. If the wrong joint is injected, the treatment cannot work. That is why PRP is recommended after a full examination and ultrasound, not on the basis of an MRI read remotely.

3. When there is a mechanical problem

A displaced meniscus that locks the knee, a loose body inside the joint or a complete tendon rupture are mechanical problems. PRP does not put a fragment back in place or join a fully torn tendon.

4. When it is used instead of exercise

In tendinopathies, the underlying treatment is progressive tendon loading. PRP can help in cases that do not respond, but it works better as part of a plan that includes exercise, not as a substitute. Without strength, the problem tends to come back. The website has phased exercise plans for the most common injuries.

5. When miracles are expected

There is no evidence that PRP regenerates lost cartilage or avoids a joint replacement that is already indicated. What studies show in selected cases is an improvement in pain and function over months. Setting expectations before starting avoids disappointment.

When it does make sense

In mild or moderate osteoarthritis that still hurts despite exercise, and in chronic tendinopathies that have not responded to a well-delivered loading programme, ultrasound-guided PRP is a reasonable option. You can read more on the pages about PRP (platelet-rich plasma) and PRP for the knee.

Frequently asked questions

Does PRP work for knee osteoarthritis?

In mild and moderate osteoarthritis, several studies show less pain and better function over months. In very advanced osteoarthritis the benefit is much smaller.

Why does PRP not work for some people?

The most common reasons are osteoarthritis that is too advanced, pain that actually comes from another structure, a mechanical problem, or not combining it with an exercise programme.

How do I know if I am a candidate for PRP?

With a full examination and ultrasound in clinic. If PRP is not right in your case, the alternatives will be explained to you.
