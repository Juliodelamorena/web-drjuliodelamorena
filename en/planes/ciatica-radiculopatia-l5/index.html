Exercise plan · Back

Exercise plan for sciatica and the lower back

A guide only. It does not replace a medical assessment or your physiotherapist's judgement. If the pain clearly increases, stop and seek advice.
Why this plan

Sciatica appears when a nerve root in the lower back becomes irritated, often the L5 root, and the pain runs down the buttock and leg. An overloaded sacroiliac joint is often added. Calming that inflammation, with time, medication or an injection, does not change the cause: a lumbar segment that, without a trunk to stabilise it, is loaded again with every long spell of sitting and every bend-and-twist. That is why sciatica comes and goes.

This programme builds that support: first control of the deep muscles, then endurance in front and side planks, and finally stability in movement. That is what prevents relapse.

The golden rule

The leg is in charge, not the back. If after a session the pain runs further down the leg or more intensely than before, you overdid it: rest the next day and start again at half. Local lower-back discomfort of up to 3 out of 10 that goes away when you finish is acceptable. Pain that centralises, moving up from the leg towards the lower back, is a good sign.

Before you start
  • Get up every 30-40 minutes. Sitting for a long time is what it tolerates worst. Walk for two or three minutes: walking does you good, so use it
  • Sleep with support. On your side with a pillow between the knees, or on your back with a pillow under the knees
  • The phases overlap. When you start a new phase, the exercises from the previous one become your warm-up. You move on by criterion: weeks without leg pain
  • Swimming and walks. From Phase 2, front crawl and backstroke on alternate days and gentle walks on the flat. Long hikes with a rucksack, from Phase 3

Phase 0 · Offloading

First days · every day · 5-10 minutes

While leg pain is high, or for five days after an injection, only gentle movements that relieve the root. If something increases the leg pain, stop it.

  1. On your back with knees bent, hands on your ribs. Breathe in through the nose, expanding the ribs sideways and the back into the floor; breathe out slowly through the mouth.

    2 × 8 breaths · every day

  2. On your back, bring your knees towards your chest with your hands, without forcing, and hold. It opens the space where the root exits.

    3 × 20-30 s · every day

  3. On your back, knees bent and together. Let them drop gently to one side and the other, without lifting your shoulders, within a range that does not hurt.

    2 × 10 · every day

  4. Sitting on the edge of a chair, straighten the knee of the affected leg while tilting your head back, and return by bending the knee while lowering your chin. A gentle movement, without holding the stretch. Only if it does not reproduce the leg pain.

    2 × 10 · every day

Phase 1 · Deep control

Weeks 1 and 2 · 5 days a week · 20 minutes

Activate the deep trunk muscles while keeping the spine neutral. Planks in short sets, prioritising technique over time.

  1. On your back, knees bent. Gently draw your navel towards your spine without moving the pelvis or holding your breath. Hold.

    3 × 10 · 10 s hold

  2. On your back, hips and knees at 90º. With the lower back pressed to the floor, lower one leg until the heel brushes the floor and return. Alternate.

    3 × 8 each side

  3. On all fours, back neutral. Extend the opposite arm and leg without the pelvis rotating, hold for two seconds and return. Alternate.

    3 × 8 each side

  4. On your back, knees bent. Lift the pelvis by squeezing the glutes until knees, hips and shoulders are in line, without arching the lower back. Lower slowly.

    3 × 12

  5. Resting on forearms and toes, body in a straight line, abdomen engaged and glutes squeezed. If the lower back sags, rest on your knees.

    4 × 15-20 s · 30 s rest

  6. On your side, resting on the forearm with the elbow under the shoulder. Lift the pelvis until the body is in line. At first, with the knees bent and resting on the floor.

    3 × 15 s each side

Phase 2 · Endurance

Weeks 3 to 5 · 4 days a week · 25 minutes

More time in the plank and control against loads that try to twist the trunk. Criterion to start: two weeks of Phase 1 without leg pain.

  1. As in Phase 1, but lowering the opposite arm and leg at the same time, without the lower back lifting off the floor.

    3 × 10 each side

  2. Standing side-on to the anchor, band at chest height. Push your hands forwards and hold for two seconds without letting the trunk rotate. Return slowly.

    3 × 10 each side

  3. Hands resting on a box or table. Push the hips back with a straight back and knees slightly bent, and return by squeezing the glutes. The right way to bend down in daily life.

    3 × 12

  4. Bridge with one leg straight, without the pelvis dropping towards the free leg.

    3 × 8 each side

Phase 3 · Stability in movement

Weeks 6 to 10 · 3 days a week · 30 minutes

The goal is for your back to cope with your activity, sport and long spells without paying for it. Criterion to start: three weeks of Phase 2 without leg pain.

  1. In a front plank, shift your body forwards and back a few centimetres from the shoulders, without the pelvis moving.

    3 × 10

  2. Move from forearms to hands, one arm and then the other, and back, with the pelvis still. Feet apart for more stability.

    3 × 6 each side

  3. In a side plank, lift the top leg and keep the pelvis high.

    3 × 20 s each side

  4. In a side plank, rotate the trunk towards the floor, taking the top arm under the body, and return by opening out. Slow and controlled.

    3 × 8 each side

Stretches · Every day

At the end of every session, from Phase 1. No bouncing and never into leg pain.

  1. On all fours, round your back bringing your chin to your chest, then arch it gently looking forwards. Slowly, without forcing the end.

    2 × 10

  2. Seated, cross one foot over the opposite knee and lean the straight trunk forwards until you feel tension in the buttock. Both sides, with more time on the affected leg.

    3 × 30 s each side

  3. Back knee resting on the floor, pelvis tucked under (tuck the tailbone in) and shift your body forwards with the arm raised. Without arching the lower back.

    3 × 30 s each side

What to avoid
  • Bending down while carrying weight and, above all, bending and twisting at the same time, as when lifting something out of the car boot.
  • Sitting for long periods without a break.
  • Swimming breaststroke or butterfly and doing tumble turns until Phase 3.
  • Long hikes with a rucksack before Phase 3.
  • After an injection, sport and swimming for the first five days.
Complementary treatment

When leg pain prevents you from doing the plan, an ultrasound-guided injection at the nerve root or the sacroiliac joint can reduce the inflammation and open the window for active work. This is assessed in clinic. No passive treatment replaces this programme.

When to seek advice before your review

  • Leg pain increases or runs further down.
  • Loss of strength in the foot or leg: tripping, difficulty walking on your heels or toes.
  • New numbness or tingling in the leg or foot.
  • Changes in bladder or bowel control, or numbness in the genital area: go to the emergency department.
  • Fever, redness or heat after an injection.

Local lower-back discomfort in the first days of each phase and ups and downs in the improvement are normal.
