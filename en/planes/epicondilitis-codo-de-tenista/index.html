Exercise plan · Elbow

Exercise plan for lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow)

A guide only. It does not replace a medical assessment or your physiotherapist's judgement. If the pain clearly increases, stop and seek advice.
Why this plan

On the outer side of the elbow, at the lateral epicondyle, lie the attachments of the muscles that lift the wrist, open the fingers and turn the palm upwards. Their common tendon works in every grip: that is why it hurts to pick up a bottle, a bag or a cup with the palm facing down, to type, to shake hands or to turn a door handle. When that tendon takes more load than it can tolerate for weeks, it stops repairing properly and becomes weaker and more sensitive.

A tendon does not heal with rest: it heals with well-dosed load, which is the treatment with the strongest support in the medical literature. Eccentric exercise (controlling the weight while the muscle lengthens) reduces pain and increases strength. Isometrics (pushing without moving the joint) ease pain on bad days and let you start loading from day one.

The plan has three phases: settle the tendon with isometrics, load it with eccentrics using bands and a flexible bar, and strengthen it with heavy, slow strength work together with the shoulder and shoulder blade, which share the load across the whole arm.

The golden rule

During the exercises, discomfort of up to 4 out of 10 in the elbow is acceptable: the tendon needs to feel the load in order to adapt. Your reference is the following morning. If the elbow is the same or better, carry on and progress. If it is clearly worse or the discomfort lasts more than 24 hours, do only the isometrics that day and go back to the previous load the next.

Before you start
  • Consistency every day. The studies that work use exercise every day for at least 12 weeks. Improvement shows at 4-6 weeks; the tendon takes three months to become resilient
  • Always slow. In the eccentrics, take 4 seconds to lower. The part that heals is the braking, not the lifting
  • Bands by colour. When all 3 sets cause discomfort of 2 out of 10 or less, move up to the next colour of band or bar: yellow, red, green and blue
  • Mind your everyday movements. Lift things with the palm facing up and the elbow bent. Avoid lifting objects with the arm straight and the palm facing down. Take breaks every 30-45 minutes when using a mouse, keyboard or tools

Phase 1 · Settle the tendon

Weeks 1-2 · every day · 10-15 minutes

If your baseline pain is low from the start, you can begin phase 2 in the second week.

  1. Seated, forearm resting on your thigh, palm facing down and wrist over the edge of the knee. Stand on the band and hold it in your hand. Keep the wrist straight, without moving it, pushing against the band. Intensity: whatever lets you hold for the whole time with discomfort of 3 out of 10 or less.

    5 × 45 s · 2 min rest · 1-3 times a day

  2. Standing, arm straight in front of you, palm facing down. Bend the wrist downwards and, with the other hand, gently draw the back of the hand towards you. A gentle pull, never pain.

    3 × 30 s · before and after each session

Phase 2 · Load the tendon: eccentrics with bands

Weeks 3-8 · every day · 15-20 minutes · 1 minute's rest between sets

This is the heart of the programme: controlling the load while the muscle lengthens is what improves pain and strength the most. Keep the isometric as a warm-up or for days with more pain.

  1. Forearm resting on your thigh, palm facing down, standing on the band. Lift the wrist with help from the other hand and, at the top, remove the help and lower slowly over 4 seconds, controlling the band with the affected wrist alone. Elbow bent at first; from week 6, with the elbow straighter.

    3 × 15 · 1-2 times a day · every day

  2. Bar in front of your chest with elbows straight. The affected hand holds one end with the wrist bent upwards; the healthy hand twists the bar. Then let the affected hand untwist slowly, over 4 seconds. Improvements of around 80 % in pain and strength have been reported with this exercise.

    3 × 15 · once a day · start with the red bar

  3. Seated, forearm supported and elbow at 90º, with the band pulling the hand inwards. Turn the palm upwards with help and return slowly, over 4 seconds, resisting the band.

    3 × 15 · once a day

Phase 3 · Strength and return to activity

Weeks 9-12 · 3 days a week on alternate days · 25-30 minutes

Keep the phase 2 eccentrics once a day on the days without strength work. Heavy, slow strength work, grip and shoulder and shoulder-blade work are added.

  1. Forearm resting on the bench, palm facing down. Lift the dumbbell over 3 seconds and lower it over 3 seconds, without help. Use a weight that lets you just reach the stated repetitions.

    Wk 9-10: 3 × 15 · Wk 11: 3 × 12 · Wk 12: 4 × 8 · 3 days a week

  2. Elbow bent to 90º and tucked into your side. Squeeze the ball with increasing force and hold. From week 11, the same exercise with the elbow straight.

    3 × 10 × 5 s · 3 days a week

  3. Elbow tucked into your side with a rolled towel, bent to 90º. Take the forearm outwards against the band and return slowly. A stable shoulder offloads the elbow with every movement.

    3 × 15 · 3 days a week

  4. Band anchored in front. Pull, taking the elbows back and squeezing the shoulder blades together, without shrugging. Return over 3 seconds.

    3 × 15 · 3 days a week

How to progress
  • Weeks 1-2. Settle the tendon: isometric 1-3 times a day and gentle stretching
  • Weeks 3-5. Start loading: eccentric with band, bar twist and supination, 3 × 15 daily, with the isometric beforehand
  • Weeks 6-8. Load more: next colour of band or bar and eccentrics with the elbow straighter
  • Weeks 9-12. Strength and movement: slow dumbbell strength, grip and shoulder 3 days a week, and a gradual return to sport or manual work
  • From week 13. Maintenance: eccentrics 3 days a week for another 3 months. This is what prevents relapses
What to avoid
  • Lifting objects with the arm straight and the palm facing down.
  • Resting the arm completely: the tendon weakens and relapse becomes more likely.
  • Moving up a band before the sets cause only low discomfort.
  • Dropping the eccentrics when you feel better: the tendon takes three months to become resilient.
Complementary treatment

When the tendon does not respond after weeks of well-executed work, ultrasound in clinic shows its condition and allows us to assess, if indicated, an ultrasound-guided injection of PRP (platelet-rich plasma). No treatment replaces loading: it works best when combined with this plan.

When to seek advice before your review

  • Tingling or numbness in the fingers, especially the ring and little fingers.
  • Sudden loss of strength or a painful snap during an exercise.
  • A swollen, hot or red elbow.
  • Night pain that wakes you or does not ease with rest.
  • No improvement after 6 weeks of doing the full plan.

Discomfort of up to 4 out of 10 during the exercises and some tiredness in the forearm are normal. Improvement shows around the fourth to sixth week and continues for three months.
