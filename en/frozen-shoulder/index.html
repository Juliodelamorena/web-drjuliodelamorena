Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis)

In frozen shoulder, or adhesive capsulitis, the capsule that surrounds the joint becomes inflamed, thickens and contracts. The shoulder hurts, especially at night, and gradually loses movement until putting on a jacket or reaching behind your back becomes difficult. It is most common between the ages of 40 and 60, in people with diabetes or thyroid problems and after a period with the arm immobilised. It does resolve, but it can take a long time; well-directed treatment shortens the process. Dr Julio De la Morena treats it at Hospital Memorial Publio Cordón (Pozuelo de Alarcón, Madrid).

Common symptoms

  • Deep shoulder pain that worsens at night and when lying on that side.
  • Loss of movement in every direction, especially when turning the arm outwards.
  • Difficulty combing your hair, fastening a bra or reaching your back pocket.
  • The arm reaches a point and stops dead, even when someone else tries to move it.

The three phases

The process goes through a painful phase, when it hurts a lot and movement starts to be lost; a stiff phase, with less pain but a locked shoulder; and a thawing phase, when movement gradually returns. Treatment changes depending on the phase you are in.

How it is diagnosed

The examination is the key: loss of external rotation, even when the doctor moves the arm, is very characteristic. Ultrasound helps rule out other causes of a painful shoulder, such as a rotator cuff tear or calcific tendinitis, and an X-ray rules out osteoarthritis.

Non-surgical treatment

  • Ultrasound-guided intra-articular injection in the painful phase, to break the cycle of pain and stiffness.
  • Capsular distension in selected cases.
  • Phased exercise, gentle and frequent: this is what restores range of movement. There is a frozen shoulder exercise plan designed for your phone.
  • Physiotherapy coordinated with the doctor.
  • Control of diabetes or thyroid problems when present.

Frequently asked questions

How long does frozen shoulder last?

Without treatment it can last between one and three years. With well-directed treatment, pain is usually controlled sooner and range of movement recovers better.

Is it good to move a frozen shoulder?

Yes, but gently: short, frequent stretches, without forcing into severe pain. Immobilising the arm makes it worse.

Does an injection help frozen shoulder?

In the painful phase, an ultrasound-guided intra-articular injection usually relieves the pain and makes movement work easier.
